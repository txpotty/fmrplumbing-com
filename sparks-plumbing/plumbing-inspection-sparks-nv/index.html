    Rental and Commercial Property Inspections in Sparks

    Landlords and property managers use our inspections between tenants or before buying an investment property. Every unit gets its own notes. We note heaters, fixtures and sewers by unit, and commercial jobs can include backflow and grease lines, giving owners a budget for repairs.

    Whole-Home Plumbing Inspections in Sparks

    Our whole-home inspections focus on the problems we see most in Nevada houses. That list starts with old supply pipe, worn angle stops, aging water heaters, slow drains and sewer lines with roots or sags. We test each one directly. Catching a corroded fitting or a partial sewer blockage now keeps it from becoming a ceiling stain or a backup on a holiday weekend.

    Pre-Purchase Plumbing Inspections in Sparks

    A house can pass a general inspection and still have a sewer full of roots. Our report tells buyers what pipe the house has, what the sewer looks like and how old the heater is, while there is still time to act. That leaves room to negotiate. Galvanized supply lines, a cracked clay sewer or a water heater past fifteen years are all things a seller can credit for or fix before closing.

    Gas Connection Safety Check in Sparks, NV

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Leaks get reported immediately. Older homes are where we find the most issues, usually at appliance connectors that have been there since the house was built.

    Pipe Material and Water Quality Review in Sparks, NV

    Pipe material says a lot about what to expect from a house. We identify copper, CPVC, PEX, galvanized, polybutylene and Kitec, and we note the known failure patterns for each one. Certain types need a plan. Poly and Kitec fail suddenly and galvanized chokes off with rust, so we flag them with replacement options.

    Preventive Maintenance Checks in Sparks, NV

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. We flush the water heater, exercise the main shutoff and angle stops, check pressure and look for slow drains and weeping fittings. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Commercial Plumbing Inspections in Sparks, NV

    Commercial buildings have equipment homes do not, so our commercial inspections cover more ground. We look at backflow assemblies, grease interceptors, commercial water heaters, flush valves and the building sewer. Inspectors like documentation. The written report helps facility managers plan and budget repairs before a health inspector or the water provider writes them up.

    Water Pressure and Supply Pipe Review in Sparks, NV

    We measure static water pressure and check the pressure reducing valve if one is installed. Too much pressure wears parts. Pressure above 80 psi shortens the life of fixtures, supply hoses and water heaters, and we also identify the supply pipe material, from copper and CPVC to polybutylene, Kitec and galvanized.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Sparks

    A standard plumbing inspection in Sparks, NV usually costs $150 to $350. Size changes the price. That fee covers the supply pipe, fixtures, water heater, drains and pressure check, while a sewer camera run is usually priced as an add-on because it takes extra time and equipment. You get the price when you book.

    Our full inspection covers supply pipe, drains, fixtures, shutoff valves, the water heater, gas connectors and the sewer line. We read static pressure. We also run a meter leak test, look for signs of slab leaks and hard water damage and check any softener or filter system tied into the plumbing.

    We inspect all the common brands and materials. That includes Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White heaters. We work with PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC, cast iron, galvanized and poly, and we know the usual weak points for each and for common fixture brands.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Camera work takes longer. Big houses, long sewers or a leak that needs tracking can stretch it, and we give you a time estimate when you schedule.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. A damp wall, a musty cabinet or a warm spot on the floor can mean a hidden leak, and an inspection finds it before it spreads.

    Plan on one to two hours for most residential inspections. Larger homes or long sewer lines can take a bit longer.

    Our plumber explains each issue, how serious it is and what the repair options are. You decide what happens next. Repair quotes are available right away or later, and you are free to hire us, hire someone else or take the report to the seller.

    Every inspection comes with a written report, photos and sewer video when included. Reports are ready the same day or the next business day.
