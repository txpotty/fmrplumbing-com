    Types of Toilet Replacements in Sparks, NV

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Sparks, NV

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more. Current WaterSense models are rated at 1.28 gallons per flush or less, and the better ones still clear the bowl in one try because the trapway and flush valve are larger than they used to be. Savings show up fast. Some water providers offer a rebate, so ask before you buy.

    Commercial Toilet Replacement in Sparks, NV

    Office and retail restrooms wear out toilets faster than any house, and a replacement there usually means a floor-mount or wall-hung bowl with a flushometer instead of a tank. We schedule around your hours. Bowls are matched to the flush valve's gallons per flush, since a mismatch leaves waste behind or wastes water.

    Commercial Flush Valve Upgrades in Sparks

    Manual flush valves on older commercial toilets leak at the handle and stick open, and swapping to a sensor valve during a replacement improves hygiene without changing the rough-in. You can pick battery or hardwired sensors. Work can happen after hours, and we check that the new fixtures meet accessibility requirements.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in Sparks, NV

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for a score of at least 800. A high score means fewer double flushes and fewer clogs, and that is where the real water savings come from.

    What a Standard Swap Includes in Sparks

    The swap itself runs about two hours: water off, tank drained, old toilet out, flange scraped and inspected, new toilet set on a fresh seal, and a braided stainless supply line connected. Then the leveling. We caulk the base except for a small gap at the back so any future leak shows up instead of hiding under the floor.

    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Sparks

    Chair height toilets put the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly two inches higher than a standard bowl, and that makes a real difference for knees and hips. No plumbing changes are needed. Many people pair it with an elongated bowl and a soft-close seat, and the swap takes the same couple of hours as any other.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Sparks

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. Some carriers need new bolts. The new bowl gets matched to the carrier already in the wall whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Gravity-Flush Toilet Replacement in Sparks

    Gravity-flush toilets are the simplest and most forgiving, and a well-made one with a 3 inch flush valve will outflush most of the older toilets it replaces. Replacement parts are on every hardware shelf. If the floor is uneven, we shim it level, then caulk the base and test several full flushes.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Sparks

    A standard toilet replacement usually runs $400 to $800 with the new toilet and labor included, depending mostly on the model you choose. Flange or subfloor repairs add to it. Those get their own price before any work, so the base number does not move.

    Replacement wins when repair costs start to approach the price of a new toilet, or when the toilet is old enough to waste water every time it flushes. A cracked tank or bowl settles the question. Frequent clogs and a rocking base point the same way.

    The toilet's outlet has to line up with the drain, and the rough-in tells you which models will do that without moving pipes. Take the measurement from the finished wall, not the trim. Most are 12 inches. If yours is 10 or 14 inches, only certain models will fit.

    A pro install costs more up front, but it comes with a flange check, the right closet bolts and a warranty if the seal leaks later. A DIY seal that leaks can quietly rot the subfloor. That repair costs far more than the install.

    It is doable for handy homeowners, but old toilets are heavy and flange problems are common once the bowl comes off. Hiring it out avoids cracked porcelain and leaks under the floor.

    Our short list starts with TOTO Drake, American Standard Champion, Kohler Highline and Gerber Viper, all of which flush well and use parts you can find at most supply houses. Mansfield makes a solid budget pick. The model gets matched to your rough-in and space.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. The age of the toilet matters too. Toilets past 25 years old are often worth replacing even if nothing is broken yet.
