    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Sparks

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Sparks, NV

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is thinner and mostly used inside the house. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Damage Assessment Before Replacement in Sparks

    We start at the meter. If the little dial on the meter is spinning with every fixture off, water is leaving somewhere. From there we listen along the line with an acoustic ground mic, check the house side for a toilet or softener running, and sometimes push a camera into the line when we suspect root damage or a crushed section, so the hole goes in the right spot.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Upgrades in Sparks, NV

    Street pressure varies a lot from one neighborhood to the next. Code says no more than 80 psi inside the house. If your new line comes in above that, we add a pressure reducing valve right after the main shutoff, set it around 55 to 65 psi, and replace the gate valve with a quarter-turn ball valve. A quarter turn and the water is off.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Sparks

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years will wear through the pipe. It costs more than PEX.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Sparks

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? We will usually just fix that spot. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so replacing the whole run is cheaper over five years. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Sparks, NV

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside when exposed to chlorinated water. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some home insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Worth replacing.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Sparks, NV

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Pulling suits softer pipe like poly or copper. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, leaving you with two small holes instead of a long trench. Rock can stop it.
