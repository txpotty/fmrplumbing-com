    Burner Tray and Venting Repair in Sparks

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look.     Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Sparks

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. Air enters through a flame arrestor screen at the base that also keeps flame inside. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. We replace any tripped thermal switch.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Sparks

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Mineral deposits crust over aerators, showerheads and valves, which is why softeners and regular water heater flushing are popular. We pull the anode, check how much magnesium or aluminum is left and replace it if the core wire is showing. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Sparks home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. That tank needs replacing.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Diagnosis in Sparks

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. That usually means a relief valve or a partly closed supply valve.
