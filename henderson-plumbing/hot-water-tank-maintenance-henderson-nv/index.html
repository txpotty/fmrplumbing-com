    Burner and Pilot Light Maintenance in Henderson, NV

    For gas hot water tanks, proper burner and pilot light operation is key for efficient heating. Our service includes cleaning the burner assembly to remove dust and debris that can interfere with combustion. We also check and adjust the pilot light, ensuring it ignites reliably. This attention to detail prevents common issues like intermittent hot water or complete heating failure, which can be stressful for homeowners in Henderson, NV.

    Electric Element and Thermostat Service in Henderson

    Electric tanks rely on upper and lower heating elements that get crusted with scale in hard water. We test each element with a meter, check the thermostats and look for scorched wires at the terminal block. If an element has failed we replace it with the matching wattage and voltage. Many families who think they need a new heater just need a lower element and a flush.

    Bradford White & Rheem Tank Maintenance in Henderson

    Pressure Relief Valve Testing in Henderson

    A properly working temperature and pressure relief (T&P) valve is a crucial safety device for any hot water tank. Our technicians will test this valve to confirm it's not stuck or leaking, preventing dangerous pressure buildup within the tank. We verify it opens and closes correctly, and if it fails, we will recommend immediate replacement to keep your Henderson home safe and compliant with plumbing codes.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Henderson

    Routine hot water tank maintenance, such as flushing and anode rod replacement, generally does not require a permit in Henderson, NV. However, if major repairs or a full replacement are needed, a permit may be required to ensure compliance with local building codes.

    Watch for slower recovery, rusty or cloudy hot water, noises, moisture around the base or a relief valve that drips. Any of these means it is time for an inspection visit.

    A typical hot water tank maintenance service generally takes about 1 to 2 hours. This includes draining, flushing, inspection and any minor adjustments or part swaps like an anode rod.

    In mineral-heavy water a magnesium rod may last three to five years. We measure it at every visit so you replace it before the tank lining begins to rust.
