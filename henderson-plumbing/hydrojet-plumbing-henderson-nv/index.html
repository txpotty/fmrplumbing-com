    Kitchen Grease Line Jetting in Henderson

    Kitchen drains collect grease that cools and hardens along the pipe wall, especially in long horizontal runs under floors and slabs. Snaking clears a path, but the grease quickly builds back. Jetting with a rotating nozzle peels that layer off and flushes it downstream. Homeowners who cook often and restaurants benefit the most from regular kitchen line jetting.

    Commercial and Restaurant Hydrojetting in Henderson

    Restaurants, hotels, apartment complexes and shopping centers rely on drains that handle heavy daily volume. Scheduled jetting helps businesses avoid backups during busy hours and stay in good standing with local pretreatment rules. We can work after closing to avoid disrupting customers.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydrojet Plumbing in Henderson

    The cost of hydrojet plumbing in Henderson, NV can vary, typically ranging from $300 to $800, depending on the severity and location of the clog, as well as the length and diameter of the pipe.     It is safe for most cast iron, clay, ABS and PVC lines in reasonable condition. We always inspect with a camera first and lower the pressure or recommend another method if the pipe is cracked or badly corroded. That step protects weak lines.

    Most homes only need jetting every few years or when clogs recur. Restaurants and busy commercial kitchens usually benefit from jetting every three to six months. We recommend a schedule based on what the camera reveals.

    We use professional cart and trailer jetters from manufacturers like Spartan and RIDGID, paired with rotating, penetrating and root-cutting nozzles. Our cameras record video of the line before and after. This equipment lets us handle both homes and commercial buildings.

    Most residential jobs take one to three hours, including camera inspections. Commercial lines with multiple runs can take a half day or more. We give you an estimate at booking.

    Rental jetters exist, but high pressure can injure you or damage a weak pipe if used incorrectly. Without a camera you also cannot tell whether the line is clean or broken. Professional jetting is safer and generally more effective.

    For most homes, hydrojetting every year or two is sufficient for preventive maintenance, especially in areas with hard water or older plumbing.
