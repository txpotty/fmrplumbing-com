    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Henderson

    Thawing an accessible frozen pipe in Henderson usually costs $150 to $400, and burst pipe repair typically runs $300 to $1,000. Prevention costs less. Insulating a crawlspace, adding heat cable to the worst run, and swapping outdoor faucets for frost-free models usually costs a few hundred dollars, which is less than a single burst pipe repair.

    For an exposed pipe you can reach, yes, carefully. Start by opening the faucet. Use a hair dryer or a space heater at a safe distance, working back from the faucet, and never use flame.

    Check which fixtures work. Ice is usually between a working fixture and a dead one. A dead sink on an outside wall usually means ice in that wall, which a thermal camera can confirm.

    You can open the faucet and gently warm an exposed pipe with a hair dryer. Open flame is never safe here. Hidden pipes are better left to us, since a hidden split can flood the wall as it thaws.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Fixing a burst usually takes a few hours. Finished walls add time for opening and cleanup, and multiple splits add more.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. The outside part drains when you close it. Leaving a hose attached defeats it, since trapped water can split the valve inside the wall.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Most homes have water back within one to two hours of our arrival. A split pipe adds time.
