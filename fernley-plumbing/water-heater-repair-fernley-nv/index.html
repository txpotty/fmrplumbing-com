    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Repair in Fernley

    Most water heater repairs run between $150 and $600. Thermocouple and element replacements sit at the lower end, while gas control valves and tankless sensors cost more. We quote the exact price before starting.

    Mineral deposits crust over aerators, showerheads and valves, which is why softeners and regular water heater flushing are popular. With hard water, once a year is a solid rule for tank heaters. Tankless units should be descaled about once a year as well, or more often if you have no water softener.

    Most standard water heater repairs, such as replacing a heating element or thermocouple, can be completed within 1 to 3 hours. More complex issues, like repairing a significant leak or replacing multiple components, might take longer, potentially up to half a day. We strive for efficient service to minimize your inconvenience.

    Noises like popping, rumbling, or hissing often indicate sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank. As the heating element warms the water, it also heats the sediment, causing these sounds. A professional flush and cleaning can often resolve this issue and improve efficiency.

    Yes, we stand behind our work. FMR Plumbing offers a warranty on all parts and labor for our water heater repairs. The specific terms and duration of the warranty will be provided to you in writing before any work begins, ensuring your peace of mind.

    Regular maintenance, including annual flushing of the tank to remove sediment, checking the anode rod, and inspecting the pressure relief valve, can significantly extend your water heater's lifespan and prevent many common issues. Ask about a maintenance plan with FMR Plumbing.

    Most repairs are finished in one visit lasting one to two hours. If a special part must be ordered, we typically have it within one to three business days.

    That smell usually comes from a reaction between bacteria in the water and the magnesium anode rod. Swapping to an aluminum zinc or powered anode and flushing the tank often solves it.
