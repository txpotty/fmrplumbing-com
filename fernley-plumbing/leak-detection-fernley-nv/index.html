    Slab Leaks From Ground Movement in Fernley

    Pipe set in concrete cannot flex when the ground moves, so it cracks at elbows and where it rises through the slab. Clay soils that swell when watered and shrink when dry can stress slabs and buried drain lines over time. We isolate the line first. Then acoustic listening and thermal imaging find the leak without breaking up the floor to look, and you get a marked spot plus repair options before any concrete gets cut.

    Gas Line Leak Locating in Fernley

    You cannot locate a gas leak by smell alone. Our plumbers pressure test the system with a gauge, and check every joint with soap and a detector. We also walk the yard line, since a dead strip of grass over a buried gas pipe can signal a leak that has run for a long time. We coordinate with the gas utility.

    Leak Testing for Multifamily and Offices in Fernley

    Multifamily buildings and office parks have long supply systems where leaks hide for a long time.     Gas Leak Detection in Fernley

    A faint sulfur smell near the range or furnace needs a professional check. Leave the house if the odor is strong. Our licensed plumbers use a combustible gas detector and soap solution on fittings, appliance connectors and the meter riser, then pressure test the line to find a leak too small to smell, and we repair it to the fuel gas code before turning service back on.

    Fixture and Appliance Leaks in Fernley, NV

    A silent toilet leak can lose hundreds of gallons a day. Worn faucet cartridges, softener valves and water heater connectors add more.     Sewer Line Leak Detection in Fernley, NV

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. Cameras find these. The camera goes in at the cleanout and runs to the main, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, with a locator giving the exact depth and distance.

    Slab Leak Detection in Fernley

We pressure test the hot and cold sides separately, then listen through the slab with a ground microphone and confirm with a thermal camera when the leak is on the hot side.
