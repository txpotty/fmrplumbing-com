    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Fernley

    Expect $75 to $200 on top of the flush for a new anode, parts and labor together. A powered anode runs higher. Seized rods and flexible link rods land near the high end.

    Many manufacturers write regular maintenance into the warranty terms and can deny a claim for sediment damage. Read the fine print on yours. A dated invoice that shows a flush and anode check each year is the simplest proof, and we leave one after every visit.

    Sediment hardens, the burner or elements work harder, and the bottom of the tank runs hotter than it should. You lose efficiency first. Then the anode runs out and the tank leaks years early.

    Popping or rumbling, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, rusty or cloudy water from the hot side, a sulfur smell, or moisture around the base. Any one is reason to call. A drip from the relief valve discharge pipe also counts, since it can mean high pressure or a failing valve.

    All the major ones, including Rheem, Ruud, AO Smith, Bradford White, State, American and Kenmore-branded tanks. Parts are easy to get for all of them. Off-brand tanks from big box stores are usually built by one of those same manufacturers, so the anode and drain valve are standard sizes.

    Rumbling is the deeper cousin of popping, and it means a thick bed of sediment is shifting and boiling on the bottom of the tank each time the burner lights. The noise grows each year. On electric tanks the scale coats the lower element.

    Our techs work on gas and electric tanks from every major maker, plus the store brands built in the same plants. Older tanks are no problem. Some tanks we see from the 1990s still have the original anode.
