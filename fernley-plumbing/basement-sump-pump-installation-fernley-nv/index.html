    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Fernley

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a good fit for narrow pits in older homes. They usually last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we generally recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Fernley

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    We install a wide range of high-quality, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you pick the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your Fernley home, considering horsepower and features.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it has to be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    Sudden summer storms can cause fast-moving floodwater that overwhelms drainage and washes debris into storm drains. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is highly recommended. It offers crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained annually, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and making sure the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and lengthens the life of your pump.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.
