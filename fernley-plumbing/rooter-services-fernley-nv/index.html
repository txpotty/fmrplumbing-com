    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Fernley

    Standard drain snaking in Fernley, NV usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Main line root cutting and hydro jetting can reach $400 to $1,000 or more. Camera work is extra. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots chase that moisture to the joint and work their way inside. Once in, they grow quickly. In Fernley, clay with joints every couple of feet and old cast iron with lead and oakum joints get hit the most.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the typical signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. Take that one seriously. Repeat clogs in one line usually mean a deeper problem, often roots at a joint well out in the yard.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Hydro jetting washes the entire inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, removing grease, scale and sludge the cable leaves on the walls. For recurring clogs, jetting is the better bet.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. Repeated use can weaken old PVC joints and pit cast iron. They are dangerous to handle. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Fernley's older homes take the most damage. They also make our job riskier. Standing water full of caustic chemicals splashes when we open the trap, so tell us if you poured anything in before we arrived.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera look can set the timing. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.
