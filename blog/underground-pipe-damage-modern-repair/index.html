    Underground Pipe Damage: Common Causes and Modern Repair Solutions

    By Luis Ortega · August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

    The Hidden Threats to Your Nevada Plumbing

    Every home faces unique challenges, and that includes the plumbing buried beneath the yard and foundation. Underground pipes, whether they carry fresh water into your home or waste away, are critical to your property's function. When these pipes fail, it's more than just an inconvenience. It can lead to significant property damage, costly water bills, and even health hazards. At FMR Plumbing, we've seen it all, from minor leaks to complete line collapses, and we understand the conditions that put Nevada plumbing at risk.

    Many homeowners don't think about their underground pipes until there's a problem. But understanding the common culprits behind pipe damage can help you recognize symptoms early and take action. Climate, soil type, and even the age of the local housing stock all play a role in the lifespan of these essential systems. Knowing what to look for and the modern solutions available can save you a lot of headache and expense down the road.

    Common Causes of Underground Pipe Damage in Nevada

    Tree Root Intrusion

    One of the most frequent offenders for underground pipe damage, especially in established neighborhoods, is tree roots. Trees naturally search for water, and the small leaks or condensation around your buried pipes can act like a beacon. Once a tiny root finds its way into a hairline crack or a loose joint, it will grow, expanding the opening and ultimately crushing or completely blocking the pipe. This is particularly common with older clay or cast iron pipes, which are more susceptible to root penetration than modern PVC.

    Shifting Soil and Foundation Issues

    Caliche, expansive clay and rocky desert soil are common in Nevada, and caliche can make digging for sewer lines slow and costly.

    Expansive clay soils, found in many parts of the country, swell when wet and shrink when dry, creating real movement beneath a home. This constant shifting can put immense stress on underground pipes, leading to cracks, dislodged connections, or even complete pipe breaks. Foundation movement, which is often related to soil expansion and contraction, can exacerbate these problems, especially for pipes running directly under or through the slab.

    Corrosion and Aging Materials

    Some homes from the 1980s and 1990s may still have polybutylene supply pipe, which is prone to sudden failures and is usually replaced.

    Many older homes were built with galvanized steel or cast iron pipes for their underground water and sewer lines. While durable in their time, these materials are prone to corrosion over decades of exposure to water and soil. Galvanized pipes can build up mineral deposits inside, restricting flow, while cast iron can rust and become brittle, making it vulnerable to cracking and collapse. Even modern PVC pipes, while resistant to corrosion, can degrade over many decades due to ground pressure or chemical exposure.

    Extreme Weather and Ground Conditions

    Intense summer heat and a dry climate are the main stresses on plumbing in Nevada, especially in the southern part of the state.

    Extreme heat can lead to soil drying out and contracting, adding to pipe stress. Severe freezes can cause pipes to freeze and burst, especially if they are not buried deep enough or are poorly insulated. Additionally, heavy rainfall and flooding can scour the ground, exposing pipes or washing away supporting soil, leaving them vulnerable to damage.

    Modern Repair Solutions for Damaged Underground Pipes

    Gone are the days when any underground pipe repair always meant extensive trenching through your yard. Modern plumbing technology offers less invasive, more efficient solutions that save time, money, and your landscaping. At FMR Plumbing, we utilize a range of advanced techniques tailored to the specific type and location of the damage.

    • Trenchless Pipe Repair: This is a big step forward for many homeowners. Instead of digging a long trench, we can often repair or replace pipes with minimal excavation. Methods include pipe lining, where a new resin-impregnated liner is pulled or blown into the existing pipe and then cured to create a new, durable pipe within the old one. This technique is well suited for repairing cracks and small breaks.
    • Pipe Bursting: For more severely damaged or collapsed pipes, pipe bursting is another trenchless option. This involves pulling a new pipe through the old one, at the same time breaking apart the old pipe as the new one is installed. It requires small access points at each end of the pipe run but avoids the need for a continuous trench.
    • Spot Repairs and Sectional Lining: Sometimes, only a small section of pipe is damaged. In these cases, we can perform a targeted repair using a process similar to pipe lining, but only for a specific segment. This is often more cost-effective and quicker than replacing an whole line.
    • High-Resolution Camera Inspections: Before any repair, accurately diagnosing the problem is crucial. We use modern sewer cameras to visually inspect the inside of your pipes, pinpointing the exact location and nature of the damage without any digging. This allows for precise, targeted repairs.

    When to Call a Professional

    Recognizing the signs of underground pipe damage early can prevent minor issues from becoming big problems. If you notice any of these symptoms, it's time to call the experts at FMR Plumbing:

    • Unexplained wet spots or lush patches of grass in your yard.
    • A sudden, significant increase in your water bill.
    • Decreased water pressure in your home.
    • Gurgling sounds from drains or toilets.
    • Slow drains or repeated backups in multiple fixtures.
    • The smell of sewage in your yard or around your foundation.
    • Puddles of water near your foundation.

    Ignoring these warning signs can lead to more serious damage, including foundation problems, mold growth, and costly water waste.
