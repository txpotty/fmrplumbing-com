    Underground Water Line Leak Repair in Reno

    Underground leaks hide well. The first clue is often a water bill that jumped for no reason.     Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Reno, NV

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Mineral deposits clog aerators, showerheads and valves, which is why softeners and regular water heater flushing are popular. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Usually a one-day job.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Reno

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, while new HDPE comes through right behind the head. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Reno

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. A shallow spot repair is cheap. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    Age is the biggest cause. Galvanized rusts and copper pits. Shifting soil, tree roots, high water pressure and freeze-thaw cycles all add stress, and an old line gets damaged by excavation work more often than most people realize. Poor original installation is another, like rocks dumped right on the pipe.

    Mineral deposits clog aerators, showerheads and valves, which is why softeners and regular water heater flushing are popular. Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks in buried supply lines often show up as warm spots on the floor or higher water bills. Tree roots wrap and crush shallow lines over time, and scale inside older pipe slowly narrows the opening until pressure drops and the wall gives out. Galvanized pipe fails most often.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. First, a cable is threaded through. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, rusty water and a climbing bill are other common clues. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. Polybutylene too, when we find it. For new work in Reno, we usually recommend Type K copper or PEX rated for direct burial, or HDPE for a trenchless pull. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.
