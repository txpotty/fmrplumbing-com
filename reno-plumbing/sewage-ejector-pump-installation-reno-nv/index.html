    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Reno

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so toilets, showers and sinks drain into a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We excavate the floor, set a basin typically 30 to 50 gallons and connect each fixture drain. A solids-handling pump lifts waste up and into the main drain line. The basin lid is sealed and vented to keep odors from escaping.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Reno, NV

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and homes with heavy below-grade use often need duplex systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. If one pump fails, the other keeps working while an alarm notifies staff. We size the basin and pumps for peak flow and install controls to code. Maintenance schedules are provided to keep both pumps in good shape.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Reno

    The cost for sewage ejector pump installation in Reno usually ranges from $2,500 to $5,000. This price can vary based on the pump type, basin size, complexity of the plumbing and electrical work and whether an existing system needs to be removed. We provide a detailed quote after assessing your project.

    Constant running, a sounding alarm, sewage odors, gurgling drains and slow drainage are typical signs. Act quickly to avoid a backup.

    Manufacturer warranties typically range from 1 to 5 years depending on the model. We also warranty our installation labor.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can influence its lifespan. Annual inspections can help extend its longevity.

    Regular maintenance for a sewage ejector pump involves occasionally checking the basin for debris, confirming the check valve works correctly and testing the float switch. It is also wise to avoid flushing non-biodegradable items. FMR Plumbing offers maintenance services to keep your pump running efficiently and prevent surprise breakdowns.

    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is built specifically to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve separate purposes.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that needs immediate attention.

    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause premature failure.
