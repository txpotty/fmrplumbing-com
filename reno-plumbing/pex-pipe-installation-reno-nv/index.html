    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Reno

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Reno

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. No crimp tool to calibrate. Because the fittings are wide inside, each joint costs very little flow.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Reno, NV

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Commercial PEX Pipe Installation in Reno

    Commercial PEX work in Reno runs on the business schedule, not ours. In a small office, a restaurant restroom or a retail space, we can work after hours and shut water down only when nobody is there to need it. Bigger sizes join the same way. Larger jobs may call for one-inch or bigger trunk lines and commercial-grade manifolds.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Reno

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Reno

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating loops need oxygen-barrier PEX, which has a coating that keeps oxygen from migrating through the wall and rusting the boiler, the pump and any iron in the system. Loop length matters as well. Too long and the far end runs cold.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Reno

    Pinhole leaks in copper rarely show up alone. Water chemistry, high flow velocity and contact with soil under a slab can all pit the pipe wall from the inside, and once a few pinholes appear, more are usually on the way. Water in much of Nevada is very hard, and scale buildup is one of the most common reasons water heaters and fixtures fail early. A switch to PEX ends it. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.
