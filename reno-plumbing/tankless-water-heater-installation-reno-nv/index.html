    Tankless heaters cost more up front than tanks, so the install has to be right. A unit with a short-cycling problem from bad sizing or a vent that ices over will spend its life throwing error codes, and the homeowner pays for that every month. We size by the numbers.

Refills are cheap.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Reno

    Conversions in Reno usually take a full day. We drain and remove the tank, upsize and test the gas, run a new vent through a wall or roof, then mount, plumb and fire the unit with service valves. Hot water is back by evening. The inspector comes after.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Reno, NV

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked. We install both condensing and non-condensing Rinnai models, and the right one depends on where the vent can go and how much you want to spend up front. The non-condensing ones need stainless vent pipe. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Cost-Effective Tankless Conversions in Reno, NV

    A tankless conversion in Reno, NV costs more than a tank swap, mostly because of the gas line upgrade, the new venting and the unit price itself. You earn some of it back each month. With no standby loss and a life of up to 20 years, plenty of homeowners notice lower gas bills within a few winters. Your quote shows the math.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Services in Reno, NV

    Businesses in Reno, NV with steady hot water use, like salons, restaurants and small gyms, often get more out of two or three tankless units racked together than one big tank. If one unit goes down, the others keep running. We size the rack by peak demand. Expect a bigger gas line and a mechanical permit on commercial jobs.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Reno

    Propane homes in Reno can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. A small tank can run short.

    Tankless vs Tank Cost Comparison in Reno, NV

    Upfront, a tankless install in Reno, NV costs more than a standard tank because of the unit price, the gas line upgrade and the venting changes. The gap shrinks over time. Less standby loss, a life of up to 20 years versus roughly 10 for a tank, and replaceable parts all count over the years. Ask for both prices.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Reno, NV

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Reno, NV homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Reno

    Gas tankless units in Reno are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.
