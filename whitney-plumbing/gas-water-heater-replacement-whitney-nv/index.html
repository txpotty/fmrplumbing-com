    Thermal Expansion Tank Installation in Whitney

    Closed systems are the reason expansion tanks exist. With a check or regulator on the main, expanding hot water has nowhere to go and the pressure spikes each heating cycle. Many codes require a thermal expansion tank there. We precharge it to your house pressure.

    No Hot Water Calls in Whitney

    No hot water at all usually points to a dead thermocouple or a gas control valve that has failed after years of service. We check those first. An old tank with a bad valve usually gets replaced.

    Gas Water Heater Efficiency Ratings in Whitney

    Efficiency ratings on tank heaters are listed as UEF, uniform energy factor, and a higher number means less gas per gallon heated. A standard tank sits around 0.60 to 0.65. Condensing models top 0.80, and some qualify for utility rebates where those are offered.

    Power Vent Gas Water Heater Installation in Whitney, NV

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust through 2 or 3-inch PVC, so they can sit far from a chimney. A standard outlet has to be within reach of the cord. Vent length and elbows stay inside the manufacturer's limits.

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Service in Whitney, NV

    Rheem tanks and their Ruud twins share most parts, which helps when a gas valve fails a few years in. We install both. Setup follows the factory manual, including the draft test.

    Big families with soaking tubs often outgrow a 50 gallon tank and need a 75 gallon heater or a high-recovery 50 at around 65,000 to 75,000 BTU. Those burners need more gas. We size the gas line before installing, since an undersized line cuts recovery.
