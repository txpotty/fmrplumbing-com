    How We Install Showers in Sun Valley, NV

    Walk-In Shower Installation in Sun Valley

    Walk-in showers depend on floor slope. With no curb, the floor needs a steady quarter-inch-per-foot pitch to the drain and waterproofing that extends past the opening. The slope is our job. We add blocking for grab bars while the studs are bare.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Sun Valley, NV

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. We pull the tub, move the drain from the end of the tub to where the shower pan needs it and raise the valve to shower height, usually around 48 inches off the floor. Slab homes need concrete work. The new shower is safer to step into.

    Emergency Shower System Replacement in Sun Valley, NV

    Water under a failed pan can rot a wood subfloor in a season. When a pan is cracked or a valve is leaking into the framing, we pull the damaged shower, check the joists and subfloor and install a new unit as quickly as the framing allows. Dry framing first. Then the new shower goes in.

    Gym and Locker Room Showers in Sun Valley

    Gym showers run back to back from open to close. Commercial valves with metered or push-button controls keep run times in check, and the drains are sized for heavy use. ADA stalls get handheld units. Work is phased so part of the room stays open.

    Shower Stall Installation in Sun Valley

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. Fiberglass and acrylic bases go down on a mortar bed so they cannot flex, and flex is the usual reason a base cracks early. The drain connects from below. We check the flow at the head once the trim is on.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Sun Valley

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Then we test. We pressure-test the valve and flood the pan for a day before tile.

    Steam Shower Plumbing in Sun Valley

    Steam showers need four things: a generator, a water supply, a drain line and an electrical circuit. We install the generator within the maker's distance limits, plumb its supply and drain and run the steam line to the head inside the enclosure. The circuit is an electrician's job. The enclosure needs a sealed door and a sloped ceiling.

    Custom Shower System Installation in Sun Valley, NV

    Multi-head showers depend on valve capacity. A standard pressure-balance valve can feed one head, but rain heads, body jets and handhelds need a thermostatic valve with separate volume controls and supply lines sized up to 3/4 inch. We plan the layout first. The water heater gets checked for capacity too.
