    PEX Water Line Installation in Sun Valley, NV

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Freezing weather is uncommon in the southern desert of Nevada, though occasional cold nights can still damage exposed outdoor pipes. PEX can swell a little in a freeze and go back to shape, where copper splits. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Sun Valley

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Sun Valley, NV

    Pipe pulling works like this. We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable or bursting head goes through the old pipe and drags new HDPE or PEX back through the same path, splitting the old pipe if it is brittle, so a driveway, a row of shrubs or a concrete walk can stay where it is.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Sun Valley

    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Sun Valley, NV

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is lighter and mostly goes indoors. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Water Line Upgrades and Code Compliance in Sun Valley, NV
