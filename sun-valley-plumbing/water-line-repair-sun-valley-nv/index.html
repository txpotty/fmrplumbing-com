    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Sun Valley

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can top $5,000 when the run is long, deep or under hardscape, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. First, a cable is threaded through. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull sometimes run two or three days when concrete has to be cut and patched or the inspector cannot come right away. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    Most jurisdictions in Nevada require a permit for a full service line replacement. Small repairs depend on the city. The permit usually comes from the city or county building department, and an inspector looks at the new pipe, the bedding and the burial depth before backfill. FMR Plumbing handles that paperwork for you.

    For PEX we use Uponor and Viega, depending on the job. Buried copper is always Type K. Couplings and transition fittings come from makers like Mueller and Ford, and we stick with fittings rated for direct burial in the soil conditions we find. Off-brand fittings stay off the truck.

    Watch the bill first. An unexplained spike usually comes before anything you can see. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, a wet spot in the yard that stays wet in dry weather, cloudy or rusty water and the sound of running water with every fixture off. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. Freezing weather is uncommon in the southern desert of Nevada, though occasional cold nights can still damage exposed outdoor pipes. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. All three work well. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX tolerates ground movement and freezing, and HDPE is the standard choice when we pull a line trenchless. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.
