    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Pahrump

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Pour a five gallon bucket of water into the pit, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. It takes a minute.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Pahrump. Replacing a pump in the same pit rarely needs one, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. We handle the paperwork.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. A new pit with slab cutting can stretch it to a full day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Our installs carry a written warranty on both parts and labor. You will see the exact length in your service agreement, and the pump manufacturer adds its own coverage, which on a quality cast iron pump usually runs three to five years from the date of purchase. We register it for you.

    A straight pump replacement usually does not need a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.
