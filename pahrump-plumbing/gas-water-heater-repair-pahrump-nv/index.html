    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Pahrump

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. The control's error code is our starting point. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Pahrump, NV

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. Plan on roughly an hour for the swap. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Pahrump

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Pahrump

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Pahrump home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    Pilot Light Repair in Pahrump

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. With a millivolt meter, it takes about a minute to see if it is putting out enough. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Pahrump

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.
