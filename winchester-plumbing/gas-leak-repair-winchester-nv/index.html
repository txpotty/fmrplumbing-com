    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Winchester

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. The first step is a pressure gauge on the piping, which tells us whether the system is actually losing gas. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final answer.

    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Winchester

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. The tag usually says what they found, so we start there. Then we test everything. After the repair, we run the pressure test the utility or inspector requires and send them the paperwork. Relights are scheduled with the utility, usually within a day.

    Commercial Gas Leak Repair in Winchester

    Commercial gas piping often runs at 2 psi or higher with regulators at each appliance bank, so a leak test there is not the same as a house test. We check regulator lockup and vent lines along with the joints. Rooftop units leak too. If a shutdown would cost you a dinner rush, we valve off just the bad branch and leave the rest live. Staff get a short briefing on the shutoff locations.

    Gas Meter to House Line Repair in Winchester, NV

    The run from the utility meter to the house shutoff is the homeowner's pipe in most service areas, so the repair falls to a plumber and not the gas company. It is also the stretch that takes the most weather, sitting in the sun or the soil. Risers rust at grade. We replace the riser or the buried run, pressure test it and leave the meter connection ready for the utility to open the meter. Expect a half day for a typical riser swap.

    Gas Line Repair & Replacement in Winchester, NV

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. We take the joint apart, clean the threads and remake it with fresh gas-rated sealant. Cracked fittings get replaced. Where a length has rusted badly, usually near a damp crawlspace vent, we cut back to sound pipe and splice in new. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a gauge test.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Winchester, NV

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. Sizing starts with the total BTU load and the longest run from the meter. Undersized pipe starves burners. Old pipe with heavy rust or bad threads gets replaced instead of patched, usually with black iron or CSST depending on access. A big tankless unit may also need a larger meter from the utility.

    Corrugated Stainless Steel Tubing Repair in Winchester, NV

    CSST is the yellow-jacketed flexible tubing used in many newer homes, and it can be damaged by a nearby lightning strike if it was never bonded. Bonding means a 6 AWG copper wire from the gas piping to the electrical grounding system. Plenty of older installs lack it. Repairs use the same brand's fittings as the existing tubing, since manufacturers do not allow mixing parts. A splice that skips that rule fails inspection.
