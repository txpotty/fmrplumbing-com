    Black Iron Pipe Repair in Winchester, NV

    Black iron is the standard interior gas pipe in a lot of homes, and it lasts a long time indoors. Joints are the weak point. Years of vibration from a furnace or a house settling can loosen threaded fittings, and pipe in a damp garage or an exterior wall can rust from the outside in. We reseal or replace the bad sections. Outdoor runs get coated or painted after the repair so rust does not start again.

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in Winchester

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. Our plumbers total the BTU load, measure the run and size the pipe so the last appliance on the line still gets full pressure at peak use. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Gas Appliance Line Installation in Winchester, NV

    A new range, dryer or water heater needs a gas line sized for its BTU rating and the length of the run. Undersized lines starve burners. Each new appliance gets a properly sized branch, a shutoff you can reach, a drip leg where required and a new coated connector. If the existing appliance line is leaking or damaged, we fix it on the same visit.

    Natural Gas Leak Detection and Repair in Winchester

    Finding a gas leak starts at the meter. We shut off every appliance and watch the meter's test dial, then close branch valves one at a time to find which section is leaking. An electronic detector does the rest. Soap solution at the suspect fitting confirms it with bubbles, and then we repair the joint, replace the section or swap the connector before testing again.

    CSST Repair in Winchester

    Corrugated stainless steel tubing is a flexible gas pipe with a plastic jacket, often yellow, that runs through attics and walls in newer homes. It can be damaged. Common damage comes from nails, rodents or lightning, and repairs require cutting out the bad section and using fittings from the same manufacturer. We check bonding on every CSST job. The system should be bonded to the electrical grounding system with a proper clamp and conductor, per the manufacturer's instructions and local code.
