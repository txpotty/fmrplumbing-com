    Commercial Drain Cleaning and Hydro-Jetting in Winchester

    Commercial drains carry grease, paper towels and food waste in volumes no house ever sees. A cable just pokes a hole. Our jetters push water at 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle that scours the full pipe wall, which clears 4-inch and 6-inch lines back to bare pipe. Jetting keeps the line open for months instead of days. We see the most benefit in restaurants, schools and multi-family stacks.

    Commercial Restroom Fixture Repair in Winchester, NV

    Restroom calls are the most common commercial work we do. Wall-hung toilets that keep clogging or rocking get a carrier check, since a loose bowl can leak inside the wall. Weak flow at one sink often comes down to a plugged stop valve.

    Backflow Assembly Testing and Rebuilds in Winchester

    Most water providers require annual tests on commercial backflow assemblies, and many send late notices that come with fines. Test early.     Grease Interceptor and Line Service in Winchester, NV

    Grease does not stay in the trap if the trap is full or the baffles are broken. It ends up in your line. Our crew checks the baffles and tees, replaces what is broken and jets the feed lines to strip hardened grease a cable cannot touch. Regular jetting means fewer backups during the dinner rush.

    Commercial Water Heater Repair and Installation in Winchester, NV

    A commercial water heater is judged by recovery rate, the gallons it can reheat per hour, not by tank size alone. Undersized heaters run cold by mid-shift. We service AO Smith, Rheem, Bradford White and State commercial units, gas and electric, tank and tankless, and we check thermostats, gas valves, flue draft and T and P valves on every call.     Slab Leak Detection for Businesses in Winchester

    A slab leak under a store or office floor often shows up as a warm spot, a running water meter with everything off, or a crack in floor tile. Caliche, expansive clay and rocky desert soil are common in Nevada, and caliche can make digging for sewer lines slow and costly. We confirm it with a meter test and pressure test, then use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak within a foot or two. Then we decide. Some leaks are best fixed with a small cut through the slab, while others are cheaper to abandon and reroute overhead. We price both so you can compare.
