    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Carson, NV

    Think of a manifold like an electrical panel, but for water lines. A central header feeds each fixture on its own line with its own valve, which lets you shut off one toilet and leave the kitchen running. It uses more pipe.     PEX for Water Line Replacement in Carson

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, often a foot and a half to three feet underground. A PEX main comes off a coil in one piece, so there are no buried joints between the meter and the foundation wall. Caliche, expansive clay and rocky desert soil are common in Nevada, and caliche can make digging for sewer lines slow and costly. It gets bedded in sand, not rocky backfill. Sharp rock against plastic pipe is how a new line fails early.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Carson

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Carson, NV

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorinated water breaks the pipe and its plastic fittings down from within, which means it can look perfectly fine until a fitting suddenly fails. Insurers ask about it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Carson, NV

    Price on a repipe depends on a short list: bathroom count, square footage, how many stories, and how easy the attic and walls are to work in. Ask and we will price all three, PEX-A, PEX-B and copper, side by side.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Carson

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.
