    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Carson

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane foam, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. They can mean the wall is bowing from soil pressure, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Carson

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin from the bottom port up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane flexes a bit if the wall shifts, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Waterproofing Costs vs. Repeat Water Damage in Carson, NV

    Paying once beats paying every two years. Waterproofing a shower wall usually lands in the low thousands, while repeat drywall, cabinet and floor repairs add up fast, and mold remediation on top of that can double the bill.     Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Carson, NV

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a two-piece clamping drain set on the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Carson, NV

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head aimed at the house instead of the bed, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Carson

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, but not counting the new tile. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, and that is what a basement sitting in wet soil or a high water table calls for.

    Schluter Kerdi, Custom RedGard and Laticrete Hydro Ban cover most of our showers. Sheet membranes like Kerdi suit a fast schedule since there is no cure time, while liquids like RedGard and Hydro Ban go on in two coats and need to dry between them. Each manufacturer's install steps are followed exactly.

    It removes the water mold needs, so new growth slows down. It does not kill mold that is already there. Any mold already growing needs to be remediated before or alongside the waterproofing, and once the space is dry and sealed it should stay that way.

    Anywhere from an afternoon to over a week. Interior crack sealing might take a day or two, while exterior excavation with new footing drains might run several days or longer than a week depending on the size of the house. Rain delays exterior work.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide since soil movement, groundwater levels and how well it was installed all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Over time, drain tile can fill with silt.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Most pumps last seven to ten years.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. Small patches we cut out and replace ourselves.
