    Compression Faucet Repair in Carson

    Compression faucets, often found in older Carson homes, are known for their separate hot and cold handles. When these faucets leak, it's typically due to worn-out rubber washers or packing. Our team is skilled in disassembling these classic fixtures, replacing the necessary seals and washers, and reassembling them to stop leaks effectively. We respect the character of older homes and make sure these repairs are done carefully.

    Dripping Faucet Repair in Carson

    A dripping spout usually means a worn cartridge, washer or valve seat. Hard water speeds up this wear by leaving scale that scratches seals and keeps them from closing tight. We disassemble the faucet, replace the worn parts and clean the valve body. Fixing a drip right away keeps a small leak from adding up on your water bill.

    Low Water Pressure Faucet Fix in Carson, NV

    Experiencing weak water flow from your faucet? This could be due to mineral buildup in the aerator, a faulty cartridge, or issues with your home's main water line. Mineral deposits crust over aerators, showerheads and valves, which is why softeners and regular water heater flushing are popular. Our technicians carefully inspect the faucet and associated plumbing to pinpoint the cause. We then clean or replace the necessary components to restore full water pressure, ensuring a steady and strong flow for daily use.

    Disc Faucet Repair in Carson

    Ceramic disc faucets are known for their durability and are a common choice in many contemporary Carson bathrooms. While generally reliable, they can develop leaks if the ceramic discs become scratched or misaligned, or if the inlet and outlet seals wear out. Our plumbers have the expertise to carefully disassemble these precise mechanisms, clean or replace the ceramic discs, and reseal the faucet to stop leaks and restore full performance.

    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in Carson

    Weak or sputtering flow is often caused by mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge. We remove and clean or replace the aerator, flush the lines and check the cartridge for scale blockage. On pull-down faucets, the spray head and check valves can also clog. If flow is low at every fixture, we test whole-house water pressure.
