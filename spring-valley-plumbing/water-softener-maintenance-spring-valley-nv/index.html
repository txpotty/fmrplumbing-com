    Control Valve and Timer Diagnostics in Spring Valley, NV

    Bypass Valve and Drain Line Repairs in Spring Valley, NV

    A bypass valve that does not seat fully lets raw water blend into the softened supply, and the hardness test is usually the first clue. The bypass gets rebuilt or swapped out and check the drain line for kinks. Code requires an air gap at the drain.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Spring Valley, NV

    Resin Rebedding in Spring Valley

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. A resin cleaner is the first thing we try. If the bed is spent, we empty out the old resin and refill the tank with new high-capacity media, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Commercial Twin-Tank Maintenance in Spring Valley

    Salons, car washes, hotels and restaurants put far more gallons through a softener than any house does, and a hard-water day can mean spotted glassware or a scaled ice machine. On twin-tank and progressive-flow units, we spend extra time on the meter and the tank switching. We service them every one to three months, depending on volume.

    Control Valve Diagnostics and Repair in Spring Valley

    Leaks at the valve body, water running to drain around the clock, a motor that hums without moving or a display stuck on one cycle tend to mean the seal and spacer stack is worn out. On a Fleck 5600 the fix is a seal kit and a new piston, often done in under an hour without pulling the valve off the tank. It costs a fraction of a new control head.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Spring Valley

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. In between, we check the salt level, the brine well and the float. Figure on an hour for a typical unit.

    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Spring Valley

    Resin does the actual softening. The beads swap sodium for calcium and magnesium, and once they are loaded up they need brine to recharge. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.
