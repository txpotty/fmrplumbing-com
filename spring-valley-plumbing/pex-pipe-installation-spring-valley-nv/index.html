    Why Choose FMR Plumbing for PEX Pipe Installation in Spring Valley, NV?

    Manufacturer rules for PEX are specific: supports about every 32 inches on horizontal runs, a minimum bend radius, and no long sun exposure. We follow them on every run. Skipping them leads to kinks, sagging lines and fittings that strain, and it can void the pipe warranty the manufacturer would otherwise honor.

    • Fewer Fittings, Fewer joints in the wall cuts the odds of a hidden drip.
    • Freeze Tolerance, PEX stretches as ice forms and often survives a freeze that would split copper.
    • Bends Around Framing, One continuous run replaces several elbows copper would need.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Spring Valley, NV

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. We match the pipe to the fittings and tools from one maker so the warranty stays intact.

    Commercial PEX Pipe Solutions in Spring Valley, NV

    Apartment buildings, small offices and churches in Spring Valley, NV are good candidates for PEX as much as houses can. Each unit can get its own manifold and shutoff, so a leak in one apartment shuts down one apartment instead of the whole stack. Fire-rated walls need care. Where PEX passes through a fire-rated assembly, we use listed firestop sleeves or sealant so the rating holds.

    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Spring Valley

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, usually 18 to 36 inches down.     PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Spring Valley

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
