    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Spring Valley

    Residential ejector systems in Spring Valley serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. The setup is a sealed basin, a pump, a float switch, a check valve and a vent. Simple parts, exact install. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Spring Valley

    Zoeller's sewage line covers home and heavier-duty use, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. Tall lifts need bigger pumps.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Spring Valley

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Snowmelt in the northern mountains can raise groundwater in spring, which affects basements and low-lying properties. Backup power can help.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Spring Valley

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Clay soils that swell when watered and shrink when dry can stress slabs and buried drain lines over time. The basin sits on gravel with backfill around it so rising groundwater cannot float it, and new concrete seals the floor and locks it down. A floating basin breaks pipe.
