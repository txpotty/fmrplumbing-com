    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Line Replacement in Summerlin South

    Most residential jobs in Summerlin South, NV land somewhere between $4,000 and $25,000. Nearly all of the difference comes from length, depth, method and whether the pipe runs under lawn or concrete. Pipe is a small share. We give you a written number after the camera inspection.

    The camera tells you. Collapsed sections, cracks you can see daylight through, long bellies holding water and joints offset by an inch or more all mean cleaning will not hold. Light roots may just need cutting.

    Backups in more than one drain at once are the big one. Gurgling toilets, a sewage smell in the yard, a soggy strip of extra-green grass and a sinking spot over the line all point the same way. A camera confirms it.

    HDPE and PVC both hold up well underground. They resist corrosion, household chemicals and roots, which matters a great deal around homes with big trees close to the sewer path. HDPE is jointless.

    Trench means one long dig along the whole line. With trenchless, we dig a pit at each end and either burst or line the pipe between them. Only digging corrects bad fall.

    Brand matters less than the standard. Our pipe comes from major makers and carries the ASTM sewer rating printed on the side. Brands change with supply.

    Properly bedded PVC or HDPE should last fifty years or more. That is a long way past old clay, which leaks at every joint, or cast iron, which rusts through along the bottom. Bedding matters most.

    Open trench leaves a strip of disturbed soil along the line. Sod gets set aside when it can be, and we compact and level the trench, though the lawn needs time to recover. Bursting means two holes.
