    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in Enterprise

    Most residential trenchless jobs run $80 to $250 per foot. Length drives it. Diameter, damage type and the method all change it, so we scope first and write the estimate from the footage.

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. Bursting replaces it. With CIPP, a resin-soaked liner cures against the old pipe wall, while bursting breaks the old pipe apart and pulls new HDPE into its place, leaving a completely new pipe.

    Lining reduces the inside diameter slightly, but the smooth surface often improves flow. Pipe bursting can maintain or increase the diameter.

    CIPP lining jobs usually take one to three days. Bursting can run longer. Pipe bursting may take slightly more, depending on length and access, but either one is much faster than open trenching, which can stretch to weeks once excavation and restoration are counted.

    Not safely. It needs special rigs. Without the training and the right gear, a handyman can damage the pipe, leave a liner that blocks a lateral or create a safety hazard, so it is best handled by a licensed plumber with trenchless experience.

    Compared to digging, yes. Far less dirt is disturbed. Trenchless repair leaves trees, plants and soil in place, and because the old pipe usually stays in the ground as the host, very little material ends up hauled to a landfill.
