    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Enterprise

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Enterprise, NV can range from approximately $300 to $1,500, depending on the type of device required, such as a DCVA or an RPZ, the complexity of the installation and any changes needed to existing plumbing. Commercial installations with larger devices may cost more. We provide a clear, upfront quote after an on-site assessment.

    Most municipalities require annual testing of backflow preventers by a licensed, certified backflow tester. This confirms the device is working correctly and continues to protect your potable water supply from contamination. We can also assist with scheduling these annual tests.

    Most residential backflow preventer installations can be finished within 2 to 4 hours. More complex commercial installations or those requiring extensive pipe changes might take a full day. We always aim for efficient service with minimal disruption to your water supply.

    Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and other equipment.

    An RPZ, or reduced pressure zone device, provides the highest level of protection and is used for high-hazard applications where contaminants could be toxic. A DCVA, or double check valve assembly, is used for low-hazard applications where contaminants are non-toxic. The choice comes down to the risk of contamination at the cross-connection point.

    We install backflow preventers from leading manufacturers known for quality and reliability. Our preferred brands include Watts, Febco, Zurn and Apollo, so you receive a durable, effective device that meets all industry standards.

    Yes, in many areas a permit is required for backflow preventer installation, especially for systems connected to the municipal water supply. FMR Plumbing will help you work through the permitting process in Enterprise and make sure all required inspections are scheduled and passed.

    Signs of a failing backflow preventer include discolored or foul-smelling water, a noticeable drop in water pressure, visible leaks around the device or water discharging from the relief valve on an RPZ. If you notice any of these, contact us for an inspection in Enterprise.
