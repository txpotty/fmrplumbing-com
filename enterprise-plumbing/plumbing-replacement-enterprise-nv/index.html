    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Replacement in Enterprise

    Costs vary by job. A tank water heater replacement typically runs $1,400 to $3,000, a water softener $1,500 to $3,500, a water line $1,800 to $5,500 and a sewer line $4,000 to $15,000 or more.

    Equipment comes with manufacturer warranties, often 6 to 12 years for water heaters and longer for some pipe materials. We also warranty our installation labor.

    The timeline for plumbing replacement depends on the complexity of the job. A water heater replacement typically takes 2 to 4 hours. A main water line or sewer line replacement can take 1 to 3 days. Whole-home repiping projects usually span 3 to 7 days, depending on the size of the house and accessibility.

    Many sewer line replacements take one to three days depending on length, depth and method. Trenchless options can speed up the timeline.

    Frequent leaks, rusty or discolored water, low pressure, recurring backups and equipment over 10 years old are common signs. We can inspect and give you an honest assessment.

    While some minor plumbing repairs can be DIY, major replacements like water heaters, sewer lines or repiping should always be handled by licensed professionals. These jobs involve complex systems, potential safety hazards (gas, high water pressure) and strict code compliance. Improper installation can lead to leaks, damage and even serious safety risks, costing more in the long run.

    Climate, freezes and soil type all affect how long plumbing lasts. The high desert in the north brings cold winters and sharp daily temperature swings that stress pipes and fittings. Freeze-thaw cycles can damage pipes, and corrosive or shifting soils can accelerate pipe degradation. Clay soils that swell when watered and shrink when dry can shift slabs and buried drain lines over time. Our replacement solutions are chosen to withstand these local conditions for lasting durability.

    We mainly use PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) and copper piping for water supply lines. For sewer and drain lines, we use durable PVC (polyvinyl chloride) or ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) pipes, depending on the application and local codes.
