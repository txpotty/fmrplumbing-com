    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Enterprise, NV

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. A bad gasket is why most of these smell.

    Commercial Grease Trap Cleaning in Enterprise

    Restaurants and hospital kitchens in Enterprise run the same basic setup, a trap or interceptor sitting between the sinks and the sewer, sized by flow rate or by gallons. The crew pumps it out and scrapes it down. Before the lid goes back on, we look over both tees and the baffle wall for cracks or missing pieces. Most kitchens land between every two weeks and every three months.

    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in Enterprise, NV

    Homes rarely have a grease trap, though a few large custom kitchens and some home-based catering setups in Enterprise, NV put a small unit under the sink. Most are 10 to 20 gallon boxes. We pull the lid, scoop out the grease and solids by hand, clean the baffle and reset the gasket so it does not leak or smell. About thirty minutes.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Enterprise

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. We pump each compartment on its own. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Enterprise

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. We lay down mats, pump or hand-scoop the contents into sealed containers, and wipe down the floor before we leave so the kitchen can go straight back to prep. Gasket and flow control get checked too.

    After-Hours Grease Trap Calls in Enterprise

    Backups do not wait for business hours. We take emergency trap calls nights and weekends. The first job is stopping the water, which usually means pumping the trap down, and the second is figuring out whether the blockage is in the trap, the outlet tee or the line downstream. Usually the kitchen opens on time the next day.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Enterprise, NV

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. A 20-gallon under-sink trap is a short visit. A 1,500-gallon interceptor left five months needs a truck, a pressure wand and disposal fees by the gallon, which puts it at the top of the range. Regular pumping keeps the per-visit price down.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Enterprise

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Other programs simply set a fixed interval, commonly 90 days for large interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.
