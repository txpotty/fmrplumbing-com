    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Mesquite

    Well pump replacement usually costs $1,800 to $4,500 depending on well depth and pump size. Deep wells or constant pressure systems can cost $4,000 to $8,000.

    Most well pumps last 8 to 15 years. Sediment, hard water and frequent cycling can cut lifespan.

    Common signs include no water from your faucets, weak or inconsistent water pressure, strange noises from the well or pressure tank area, short cycling where the pump turns on and off frequently, or cloudy or muddy water. If you notice any of these, it is time to call a professional for inspection.

    It is often a good idea to consider replacing your pressure tank at the same time as your well pump, especially if both are of similar age. A new pressure tank can improve the efficiency and lifespan of your new pump by reducing cycling. It also saves on labor costs to do both jobs at once.

    Permit requirements vary by county. We follow local requirements for well work.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job involving electrical wiring, heavy lifting and specialized tools, especially for submersible pumps. It is strongly recommended to hire a professional plumber for safety, proper installation and compliance with all codes, preventing costly mistakes.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, usually less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has specific applications and benefits.

    Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is typically 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water. Deep well pumps, or submersible pumps, are placed directly in the water near the bottom of the well and push water up, suited for depths over 25 feet. Choosing the correct type is critical for efficiency and performance.
