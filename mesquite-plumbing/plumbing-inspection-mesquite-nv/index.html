    Thorough Plumbing Inspections in Mesquite, NV

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in Mesquite

    Water in much of Nevada is very hard, and scale buildup is one of the most common reasons water heaters and fixtures fail early. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. We look for crusty aerators, scaled showerheads, slow-filling toilets and sediment in the water heater, and we can run a simple hardness test on the spot. Then we explain your options. Softeners, filters and scale reducers all help, and we suggest the one that matches the reading we took.

    Gas Connection Safety Check in Mesquite, NV

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Leaks get reported immediately. Older homes are where we find the most issues, usually at appliance connectors that have been there since the house was built.

    Seasonal Plumbing Checks in Mesquite, NV

    Water heaters in hot garages wear out sooner, and outdoor pipes and irrigation lines take a beating from sun and heat. A seasonal check looks at the parts of the system that weather affects most. Outdoor faucets, exposed pipe, irrigation backflow devices and the heater drain and relief valve all get checked. We look at pipe insulation. Swapping a cracked outdoor faucet or insulating a pipe costs little next to a burst line in the wall.

    Water Heater and Softener Inspection in Mesquite

    We check the water heater's age by serial number, look for rust, leaks and sediment and test the relief valve. Venting and gas connections get checked. Expansion tanks, drain pans and softeners get a look as well, and a quick hardness test tells us whether the softener is actually doing its job or just using salt.

    Leak Detection and Assessment in Mesquite, NV

    Hidden leaks waste water and quietly damage floors and walls. Start at the meter. With all fixtures off, we watch the meter's leak indicator, then use acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera to trace the leak behind walls, under floors or in the slab. Early detection limits the damage. Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks in buried supply lines often show up as warm spots on the floor or higher water bills.

    Pipe Material and Water Quality Review in Mesquite, NV

    Pipe material says a lot about what to expect from a house. We identify copper, CPVC, PEX, galvanized, polybutylene and Kitec, and we note the known failure patterns for each one. Certain types need a plan. Poly and Kitec fail suddenly and galvanized chokes off with rust, so we flag them with replacement options.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Mesquite

    Standard plumbing inspections usually cost $150 to $300. With the sewer camera, most totals land between $250 and $500.

    Most residential inspections in Mesquite take one to two hours. Older or bigger homes take longer. The exact time depends on how big the house is, how old the plumbing is and whether we find something that needs extra tracing, such as a hidden leak or a sewer defect.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It has limits. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    Our technicians inspect all major water heater brands, including Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White. Tankless units are covered. Rinnai and Noritz tankless units and most household fixture brands are routine, so your equipment brand will not be a problem.

    Most homes do well with an inspection every one to two years. Buying a home is another time. If you are purchasing a property, live in an older house or have had recent leaks or backups, a yearly check gives you a better chance of catching the next problem early.

    No permit is needed for an inspection. Permits apply to the work. If the inspection leads to repairs or replacements that need a permit, we will say so up front and handle it if we do the job.

    For an older home, it usually pays for itself. Old pipe hides problems. Homes with galvanized supply pipe, clay or cast iron sewers or original water heaters often have problems that are invisible from the outside, and an inspection lets you plan repairs instead of reacting to a failure.
