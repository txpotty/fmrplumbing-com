    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Las Vegas, NV

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. Every compartment gets opened and pumped to the bottom. With the tank empty, we inspect the tees, baffle wall, risers and lid seals, since a cracked riser lets dirt and groundwater into the tank. Lids go back on tight.

    Commercial Grease Trap Pumping in Las Vegas

    Fryer-heavy menus load a trap fastest. Two weeks is enough for a wings place to fill a 50-gallon trap. The trucks have the hose length for traps behind walk-ins or in basements, plus tank room for interceptors over 1,000 gallons. Each pump-out goes to the bottom.

    Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Las Vegas

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. Mats go down first, the contents get pumped or scooped into sealed containers, and the floor gets wiped so prep can start right after. Gasket and flow control get checked too.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Las Vegas

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Other programs simply set a fixed interval, commonly 90 days for large interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.

    Compliance Documentation for FOG Programs in Las Vegas, NV

    Inspectors usually ask for at least a year of pumping records. Missing records can count as a violation even if the trap is clean. Each sheet shows the date, gallons, measured depths and hauler name, and copies can go by email to your manager or the corporate office after each visit. Keep the originals on site.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Las Vegas, NV

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. A bad gasket is why most of these smell.

    Grease Trap Hydro-Jetting in Las Vegas, NV

    Some traps have years of grease baked onto the walls and inlet piping. Pumping alone leaves that crust in place. We jet the inside of the empty trap and the line feeding it, which takes the unit back to bare walls and restores the capacity the crust was eating up. Plan on about an extra hour.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Las Vegas, NV

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly. A 1,500-gallon interceptor that has gone five months without pumping needs a vacuum truck, a pressure wand and extra disposal fees for the volume, so it lands at the high end. A steady schedule lowers what each visit costs.
