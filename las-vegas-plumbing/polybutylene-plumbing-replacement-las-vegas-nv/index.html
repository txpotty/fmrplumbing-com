    Polybutylene Replacement Services in Las Vegas, NV

    PEX Pipe Installation in Las Vegas, NV

    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Faster install means lower labor cost. Attic PEX stays out of UV, clear of flues and can lights and supported as the maker specifies, which protects the pipe and the warranty.

    Full Home Polybutylene Repiping in Las Vegas

    A full replacement takes every poly supply line out of service, not just the sections that have failed. Partial jobs leave risk behind. We run new lines from the main shutoff to every sink, toilet, tub, washer, ice maker and water heater, add new quarter-turn stops and test the whole system before patching.

    Yard Service Line Replacement in Las Vegas, NV

    Many poly-era homes also have a blue or black polybutylene service line running from the water meter to the house. These buried lines can split under the yard, raising the water bill and soaking the soil. Utilities get marked first. After locating, we trench, lay new PEX or copper and tie it into a new main shutoff at the house.

    The Cost of Polybutylene Replacement in Las Vegas, NV

    Price depends mostly on house size, the number of bathrooms, attic access and whether you pick PEX or copper. PEX is the cheaper option. One burst can cost more in drywall, flooring and cabinets than the full replacement, not counting your deductible. Every estimate breaks out labor, pipe, permit and drywall.

    Polybutylene to PEX Transitions in Las Vegas, NV

    Sometimes the yard service line coming into the house is not polybutylene, or it was replaced already. In that case, we connect the new PEX system to the existing service at the main shutoff with an approved transition fitting, which avoids digging up the yard. The service line gets checked. If it is blue or black poly, we will recommend replacing it too.

    Polybutylene Leak Repair in Las Vegas

    Repairs on polybutylene are temporary by nature, because the pipe next to the repair is the same age. We still make them. When a poly line leaks, we cut out the bad section and splice in PEX with approved transition fittings, which stops the leak and gives you time to schedule a full replacement on your own terms.

    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Las Vegas

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. The meter often tells first. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random. Once we find it, a temporary fix buys time to schedule the repipe.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Las Vegas

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Water in much of Nevada is very hard, and scale buildup is one of the most common reasons water heaters and fixtures fail early. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Las Vegas

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines often add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. In water that does not attack it, Type L copper can last 50 years or more, and both are major upgrades over poly.

    A full poly repipe in Las Vegas, NV usually takes three to seven days. Bigger homes take longer. We give you a clear day-by-day timeline at the estimate, including which rooms we will work in each day and when the drywall finish will happen.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It is not a weekend job. Hidden leaks, failed inspections and voided warranties are common results of DIY repipes, so this one is best left to licensed plumbers.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. Your house and water decide which we suggest.

    Our poly replacements carry a written warranty on materials and labor. Details are written up front. PEX-A jobs done by certified installers also get the maker's long-term warranty, which we register on your behalf.
