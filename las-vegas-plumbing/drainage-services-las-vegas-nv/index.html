    Roots grow toward the water and nutrients inside sewer pipe. A hairline crack is enough. Once a root tip gets in through a cracked joint or loose seal, it grows into a mass that catches paper and grease, and clay and older concrete pipe with many joints are the most vulnerable.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains are harder. A French drain or dry well needs correct slope, the right fabric and stone, and an outlet that will not dump water on a neighbor, and digging without an 811 locate risks hitting a gas or power line.

    Store-bought cleaners rarely clear a real clog. They often make it worse. They sit in the trap, can damage pipe and joints, and leave caustic liquid for the plumber. A cable is safer.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It is fast and inexpensive. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Larger projects run longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.
