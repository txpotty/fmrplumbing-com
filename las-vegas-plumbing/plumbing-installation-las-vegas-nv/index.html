    Water Heater Installation in Las Vegas

    Tank or tankless, gas or electric, the first question is how many people shower in the morning. We size the heater to that peak demand and your fuel source, then check the venting and gas supply before quoting. Gas heaters need the closest look. A draft hood that spills or a flue with the wrong pitch can push carbon monoxide back into the house. We haul the old tank away before we leave.

    Tank and Tankless Water Heater Installs in Las Vegas

    Bradford White and Rheem both make good tanks, and the brand matters less than the fit. We look at hot water demand, the space you have and whether gas or electric is cheaper to run at your house. Then we check the Nevada code items. On a closed system with a check valve or pressure reducing valve, an expansion tank is usually required, and we set it with the heater.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Las Vegas, NV

    Electric tankless heaters pull serious amperage, often 120 amps or more for a whole-house unit. Before we quote one, we check that your panel and service can carry that load, and we bring in a licensed electrician when they cannot. The plumbing side is simpler. We set the flow rate and outlet temperature so the shower does not run cold when the dishwasher kicks on.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Las Vegas, NV

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. The fix is a sealed basin with an ejector pump that lifts waste to the main. Pump and basin are sized to the fixtures, the basin is vented to code, and the discharge gets a check valve and a shutoff so the pump can come out for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    Water Heater Replacement in Las Vegas

    Most tank heaters give out between 8 and 12 years, sooner with heavy sediment. We install 40 and 50 gallon tanks, plus 75 gallon units for big households, from Rheem and Bradford White, with new flex connectors, a relief line piped to code and a vent check on every job. We also check whether you need an expansion tank. Most installs finish in one visit.

    Sump Pump and Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Las Vegas

    Basements and crawl spaces that take on water need a sump pump, and fixtures below the sewer line need an ejector. They are different pumps for different water, and one cannot stand in for the other. We size before we sell. The pump gets matched to the fill rate, wired to a dedicated circuit and piped out through a check valve.

    Gas Line Installation for New Appliances in Las Vegas, NV

    Water heaters, ranges, dryers, fireplaces and pool heaters all pull from the same gas service. Each branch is sized from the total BTU load and the run length so the last appliance on the line is not starved. New lines get pressure tested, always. A load that outgrows the meter needs an upgrade, which we coordinate with the gas company.

    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Las Vegas, NV

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. An expansion tank absorbs that swing and keeps the relief valve from dripping. A PRV usually means you need one. For basement baths and guest suites below the sewer, we also install ejector systems with a sealed basin, check valve and vent.
