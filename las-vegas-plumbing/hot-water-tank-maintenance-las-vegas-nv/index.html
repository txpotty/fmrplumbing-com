    Electric Element Check in Las Vegas

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Emergency Tank Inspections in Las Vegas

    Sudden changes call for a look the same day: a tank that suddenly rumbles, hot water that turns brown, a puddle forming under the pan, or a relief valve that will not stop dripping. Diagnosis comes first. Sometimes the fix is a flush and a new drain valve, and sometimes the tank is leaking from the liner and nothing will save it.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Las Vegas, NV

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. A tired thermocouple drops the pilot, so we test it too. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Las Vegas

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Las Vegas

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. Those chunks cause the popping. When a plain flush will not move them, we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems, let it sit and then flush it all out. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Las Vegas, NV

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. Attic tanks need a working pan. We check that the pan drain line runs to a safe spot and is not cracked or plugged, because one slow leak over a bedroom can bring down a ceiling. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Las Vegas, NV

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. On newer FVIR models, garage dust and dryer lint can clog the flame arrestor screen at the bottom, which starves the burner and makes the pilot drop out. We vacuum it clean.

    Electric Thermostat Service in Las Vegas

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. When the reset trips, something caused it. We find out why it tripped, which usually comes down to a shorted element or a thermostat stuck closed, before we press it back in. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.
