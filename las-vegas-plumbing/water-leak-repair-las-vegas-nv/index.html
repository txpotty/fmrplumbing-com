    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Leak Repair in Las Vegas

    Simple fixture leaks often cost $150 to $400, while in-wall leaks run about $300 to $900 before patching and painting the drywall. Slab and yard line leaks typically range from $1,000 to $4,000. Pricing is set once the leak is found.

    Copper, PEX, PVC, CPVC and galvanized steel are all common in Nevada homes of different ages, and we repair each one. We also repair Moen, Delta, Kohler and American Standard fixtures.

    Most policies cover sudden water damage and sometimes the cost to access the leak, but not the pipe repair itself. We provide detailed documentation to help with your claim.

    Age and corrosion cause most leaks, along with soil movement and high water pressure. The northern part of the state sees regular hard freezes, so pipes in exterior walls and crawlspaces need protection. Hard water scale can also wear pipes down over time.

    Sudden, accidental damage like a burst pipe is often covered. Leaks from neglect or long-term corrosion may not be, so check with your insurance company about your policy.

    Repeat pinholes often come from water chemistry, high pressure or thin-walled copper. We test your pressure, inspect the regulator and talk about repiping if leaks keep returning.

    Insulate exposed pipes before cold weather and keep house pressure under 80 psi. Fix small drips right away, and consider replacing old corroded pipe with PEX.

    Turn off every fixture and watch your water meter. A moving leak indicator means water is going somewhere. High bills, warm spots on tile floors and musty odors in a closet or under a sink are other common signs of a hidden leak.
