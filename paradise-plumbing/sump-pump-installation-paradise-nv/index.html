    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Paradise

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi monitors go further and send a phone alert when the pump runs too long, the power drops or the water climbs past a set level. Landlords like them. They can go on a new pump or one that is already working.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Paradise

    Submersible pumps live at the bottom of the pit with a sealed, oil-cooled motor. They run quieter and move more water. The pit lid sits flush. We seal the cord grommet and the lid on every install.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Systems in Paradise, NV

    Backup systems we install use sealed AGM batteries that do not need water. A smart charger keeps the battery topped off and sounds an alarm if the battery weakens, the primary fails or the water rises past the backup float. Expect a battery swap every four to five years. Finished basements get the most out of them.

    Crawl Space Sump Pump Installation in Paradise, NV

    Crawl spaces with standing water breed mold and rot sill plates within a few seasons. A small sump basin at the low point fixes that. Clearance is usually under two feet. We route the discharge out through the rim joist and away from the foundation.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Paradise, NV

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. Our trucks carry 1/3 and 1/2 HP submersibles, check valves and fittings, so in most homes we can pull the old pump and set a new one in one visit. Figure on two hours for a typical swap. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    Liberty Pumps Installation in Paradise, NV

    Liberty builds quiet, well-made submersible pumps with good switch options. We install their 250 and 280 series often. Parts are easy to find. We drill a 3/16 inch weep hole in the discharge to stop air lock.

    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Paradise

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. Because everything comes assembled on a single riser, they fit into an 18-inch pit that would be too tight for two separate pumps and two check valves. That saves some labor. They suit homes that want backup protection but do not want to enlarge the pit.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Paradise

    No pit yet? We saw-cut the slab at the low corner, break out the concrete and dig down through soil, hardpan or rock to set an 18 by 22 inch basin. We backfill around the basin with washed gravel so groundwater has a path in. The slab gets patched with new concrete. Last come the pump, the lid and the discharge line.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Paradise

    Sump pump installation in Paradise, NV runs about $800 to $2,500 for most homes. The biggest swing comes from whether we are swapping a pump in an existing pit or cutting a new pit through the slab, which adds concrete work and several hours of labor. Backups add to it. You get a written price after we look at the space.

    Not every home needs one. Homes with basements, crawl spaces, sunken patios or yards that collect runoff get the most from a pump, since storm water has nowhere else to go.

    Submersibles are quieter and stronger, so they suit heavy use. Pedestal pumps cost less and are easy to service, though they are louder.

    Our trucks carry Zoeller, Liberty, Wayne and Flotec pumps. We choose based on lift, inflow and budget.

    A submersible sits in the pit under water, so it runs quieter and takes no floor space. A pedestal pump keeps the motor on a column above the pit. Both work. The pit and the noise you can live with decide it.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two or three hours. A new pit can take a full day.

    Permits depend on the scope of the job and the local building department. Plumbing contractors must be licensed by the state contractors board, and the state takes unlicensed work seriously. FMR Plumbing handles permits and inspections in Paradise.

    Doing it yourself is possible but risky. Getting the pit depth, the check valve orientation, the discharge route and the outlet wiring right takes experience, and one mistake shows up during the next storm. We do it daily.
