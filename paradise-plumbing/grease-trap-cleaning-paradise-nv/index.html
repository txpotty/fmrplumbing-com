    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Paradise

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. The biggest variable is the gallons hauled, because disposal facilities charge by volume and a 1,500-gallon tank is a full truckload.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. Warm, it pours like water, and cool, it sets like wax. In a sewer pipe it sticks to the walls in layers, catches food scraps and wipes, and eventually closes the pipe down to a pencil-sized opening.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. Disposing of it is where people get in trouble. Pretreatment programs often require a licensed hauler's record for each pump-out, so a kitchen that handles its own cleaning may have no paperwork to show when the inspector asks.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Plenty of busy restaurants pump monthly. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    Zurn, Schier, Watts and Big Dipper units turn up across Nevada every week, along with Rockford traps and older steel boxes from makers that are long gone. Parts are usually available. For discontinued units we can often fit a universal baffle or flow control, or quote a replacement if the body has rusted through.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Outdoor interceptors run one to three hours. The long end is for big tanks with a hard grease cap, where the crew has to break up the cap with a pressure wand before the suction hose can move it.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Longer term, a hardened grease cap that can take hours to break up. Hydrogen sulfide from the rotting grease also attacks concrete, so interceptors that go unpumped for years often need new baffles and risers well before their time.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. Those AGRUs have a heater and a skimmer wheel that pull grease into a container, and they need the container emptied and the wheel and screen cleaned on a schedule.
