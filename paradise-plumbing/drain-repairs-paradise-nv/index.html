    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Paradise

    Simple trap or slip-joint repairs usually cost $150 to $350. Replacing a section of drain pipe usually runs $400 to $1,200, and cast iron stack replacement can range from $2,000 to $6,000 depending on access. We provide a written price before starting.

    Tightening a loose slip nut or replacing a plastic trap is a fair DIY job. If the leak is from a cracked tailpiece, a corroded metal drain or inside a wall, a plumber can make a lasting repair and check for hidden damage.

    Minor clogs can sometimes be resolved with plungers or enzymatic drain cleaners. However, for stubborn blockages, recurring issues or suspected pipe damage, it is best to call a professional. DIY attempts with harsh chemical cleaners can sometimes damage pipes or mask a larger problem.

    Hydro-jetting is a powerful drain cleaning method that uses high-pressure water to scour the inside of pipes, thoroughly removing stubborn clogs, grease buildup and even tree roots. It's typically used when traditional snaking isn't enough, or for preventive maintenance on main sewer lines, especially in older homes with buildup issues.

    Harsh chemical cleaners can soften older ABS glue joints and corrode metal drain parts with repeated use. They also seldom clear grease or scale fully. A mechanical auger or hydro-jet is safer and more effective.

    To prevent future drain problems, avoid pouring grease down sinks, use drain screens to catch hair and food particles and be mindful of what goes down your toilets. Regular professional drain cleaning every one to two years can also help head off clogs and extend the life of your plumbing system.

    Minor drain repairs, such as clearing a simple clog, typically do not require a permit in Paradise. However, significant repairs involving altering or replacing major sections of your plumbing system, especially main sewer lines, may require permits. FMR Plumbing handles all necessary permit applications for these projects.

    You should call a professional if your DIY efforts with a plunger or basic snake haven't cleared the clog, or if you have recurring clogs, foul odors or multiple slow drains. These issues usually point to a deeper problem that requires specialized tools and expertise to resolve effectively.
