    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Paradise, NV

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. Swapping the rod usually fixes it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Paradise, NV

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Most rods last three to five years. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.

    Electric Element Check in Paradise

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Paradise, NV

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. Draft at the hood gets checked first. On newer FVIR models, garage dust and dryer lint can clog the flame arrestor screen at the bottom, which starves the burner and makes the pilot drop out. We vacuum it clean.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Paradise

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve.     T&P Valve Replacement in Paradise

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. We match the new valve to the heater's input rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Relief Valve Testing in Paradise

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Paradise

    Most flush and inspection visits in Paradise land between $100 and $250, depending on tank size and how hard it is to reach. An anode swap is extra. You hear the flat price before we schedule.

    Less flushing, yes, since softened water leaves far less scale. The anode wears faster, though. Sodium makes the water more aggressive on magnesium rods, so a softened home still needs a yearly visit, mostly to keep an eye on the anode rather than the sediment.

    Water in much of Nevada is very hard, and scale buildup is one of the most common reasons water heaters and fixtures fail early. Dissolved calcium and magnesium drop out as scale every time the water heats, and that scale settles on the tank floor or crusts onto electric elements. Every layer costs you energy. A twice-yearly flush makes sense where water runs hard.

    A DIY flush clears loose sediment and stops there. We pull and measure the anode. We also lift the relief valve, gauge the expansion tank and house pressure, check draft or element resistance, and look at every fitting for corrosion, which is where most early warnings show up.

    Yearly, for most homes. Every six months is better on very hard water. Most owner manuals call for a yearly flush, and records help with warranty claims.

    Popping or rumbling, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, rusty or cloudy water from the hot side, a sulfur smell, or moisture around the base. One of those is enough. A drip from the relief valve discharge pipe also counts, since it can mean high pressure or a failing valve.

    Sediment hardens, the burner or elements work harder, and the bottom of the tank runs hotter than it should. You lose efficiency first. After that the anode runs out, corrosion starts on the liner, and the usual ending is a leak from the bottom seam on a tank that might have lasted another five years.

    Once a year is the baseline almost everywhere. Hard water pushes that to every six months. Homes with a softener and light use can sometimes stretch it, but we still want to see the anode every year.
