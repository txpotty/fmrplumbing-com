    Sewer Camera Inspection for Trenchless Repair in North Las Vegas

    Every trenchless job starts with a push camera fed in from the cleanout or a pulled toilet.     Commercial Trenchless Sewer Lining in North Las Vegas, NV

    Office and retail buildings often have a cast iron building drain under the slab that has rotted along the bottom.     Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Pricing in North Las Vegas, NV

    Compare total cost, not just the plumbing line on the invoice.     Root Intrusion Repair in North Las Vegas, NV

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once in, they fill the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. With no joints left, there is nowhere for them to return.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Upgrades in North Las Vegas

    Bursting can upsize the line. We can pull in 6-inch HDPE behind a head that bursts an old 4-inch clay pipe, which helps on lines that clog because they were undersized from the start or have been added onto with extra bathrooms. Each end only needs a pit around three by four feet. No trench in between.

    Emergency Trenchless Sewer Repair in North Las Vegas

    Sewage coming up through a floor drain is an emergency, but the first fix is usually a cable or a jetter to get the line flowing again. Lining comes after that. FMR Plumbing can often schedule the liner within a few days. In the meantime the house drains normally.

    Sectional Point Repairs in North Las Vegas

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. It rides in on an inflatable packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. It fixes a single crack or root entry, not a whole failing line. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Lining in North Las Vegas, NV

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. Plumbers call that channel rot, a groove that eats through the bottom. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.
