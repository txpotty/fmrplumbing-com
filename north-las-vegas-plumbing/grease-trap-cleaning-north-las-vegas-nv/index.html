    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in North Las Vegas

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. Disposal is billed by the gallon at the receiving facility, so the gallons hauled drive most of the cost, and a 1,500-gallon tank fills a truck.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. The waste still needs legal disposal. A common setup is daily skimming by staff and a full pump and scrape from us every few weeks, so the records stay complete.

    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. The 25 percent rule shows up in most of them. Plenty also cap the interval, want a year or more of records on site, and ban enzymes in place of pumping, so we look up your program before we schedule.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. Disposing of it is where people get in trouble. Many programs want a licensed hauler's record for every cleaning, and a kitchen doing its own has nothing to show the inspector.

    Yes, every visit. You get the date, the gallons and the depth readings. Baffle and tee condition, the hauler and the receiving facility are on it too, which matches what most inspectors ask for.

    Everything in the trap comes out, from the floating grease cap down to the sludge of food solids on the floor. The industry calls it brown grease. It is different from the yellow grease in your used fryer oil bin, which is recycled separately and should never be poured into the trap.

    FOG stands for fats, oils and grease. Warm, it pours like water, and cool, it sets like wax. In a sewer pipe it sticks to the walls in layers, catches food scraps and wipes, and eventually closes the pipe down to a pencil-sized opening.

    It is the most common pumping standard. Pump before grease and solids fill a quarter of the depth. Beyond that level, water passes through too quickly to separate, and grease leaves through the outlet even though the trap seems fine.
